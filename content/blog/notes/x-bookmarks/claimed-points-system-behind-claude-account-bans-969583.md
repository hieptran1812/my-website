## Summary

@make_claude says the Chinese community has reverse-engineered how Claude bans accounts. The claim is that each account gets a starting score at registration, gains or loses points based on behavior, and is banned once it falls below a threshold. The quoted post is a long personal write-up framed as a record of how people pay for and build their AI tool stacks today.

## Background

AI providers run trust-and-safety systems that flag accounts for abuse, payment fraud or policy violations. These systems are not public, so outside descriptions of them are guesses based on observed patterns. A score-based model is a common design for fraud and abuse detection in general.

## What I took away

- Treat reverse-engineered descriptions of a provider's ban system as unverified claims.
- Avoid account behavior that looks like reselling, sharing or evading regional limits if you depend on a paid AI account.
- Keep a fallback provider so one account ban does not stop your work.
