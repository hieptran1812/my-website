## Summary

The author says spot flow is often seen as cleaner since it is unleveraged demand, while moves driven by perpetual futures are often dismissed as fake because of leverage and funding. He says this is mostly accurate but needs an important nuance. The quoted post is a CVD divergences cheat sheet explaining that CVD tracks net aggressive market buys minus sells.

## Background

CVD (cumulative volume delta) adds up market buy volume minus market sell volume over time. A perpetual future is a futures contract with no expiry, kept near spot price by funding payments. Comparing spot and perp flows is an educational way to study who is driving a move. This is not trading advice.

## What I took away

- CVD measures aggressive orders, not just total volume.
- Spot and perp flows can tell different stories about the same price move.
- Treat these indicators as one input, not a signal to act on.
