## Summary

The author says they have the same background and finds kernels an elegant way to represent distributions through kernel mean embedding. Their current focus is hyperdimensional computing, which they call even simpler but built on the same ideas.

## Background

A kernel mean embedding maps a whole probability distribution to a single point in a feature space. Hyperdimensional computing represents data as very long random vectors and combines them with simple operations. Both rely on high-dimensional vectors to capture structure.

## What I took away

- Different fields reuse the idea of representing objects as high-dimensional vectors.
- Hyperdimensional computing is simple but rooted in kernel methods.
- Kernel mean embedding is worth learning for distribution comparison.
