## Summary

The author sets up an ML interview question. Two models are 88% accurate: Model A is 89% confident and Model B is 99% confident. Saying either would work is presented as the wrong answer, with an explanation to follow.

## Background

Calibration means a model's confidence matches how often it is right. Model A is well calibrated, as 89% confidence with 88% accuracy is close. Model B is overconfident, which can be risky when decisions depend on its probabilities.

## What I took away

- Accuracy alone does not tell you how reliable confidence scores are.
- Prefer the better-calibrated model when probabilities drive decisions.
- Calibration can be measured and sometimes fixed after training.
