## Summary

The author says economists may be waking up to computational methods, and recalls trying to get macro friends to take agent-based models seriously for studying fiscal policy. The quoted post asks whether AI can learn economic states despite the Lucas Critique. It simulated data from a New Keynesian model, fit a transformer, and found it generalized surprisingly well out of sample.

## Background

The Lucas Critique says relationships learned from past data may break when policy changes. Agent-based models simulate many individual actors to see aggregate effects. The quoted work tests whether a transformer can learn the model's dynamics from data.

## What I took away

- Learned models are being tested on classic economics problems.
- Generalization out of sample is the key test.
- The result is on simulated data, not real economies.
