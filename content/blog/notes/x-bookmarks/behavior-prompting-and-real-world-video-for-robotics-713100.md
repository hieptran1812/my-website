## Summary

The quoted post introduces Behavior Prompting: a human shows one demo and the robot adapts immediately, using robot demos as in-context prompts without paired human-to-robot data. The author says this shows how important real-world video data is for robotics. He adds that some companies in India put cameras on factory workers' heads to record repetitive tasks; the text cuts off.

## Background

In-context learning lets a model pick up a task from examples in its input instead of retraining. Applying it to robots means a demonstration acts like a prompt. Collecting human video is a cheap source of robot training data, but raises worker consent and privacy issues.

## What I took away

- Demonstrations can serve as prompts for robot policies.
- Real-world video is a key data bottleneck for robotics.
- Data collection from workers raises ethical questions worth considering.
