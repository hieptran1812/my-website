## Summary

@GoshawkTrades lists possible mistakes: testing a single instrument, overcomplicating strategies, not focusing on inefficient markets, focusing too little on exits, and not combining weaker signals into an ensemble. It answers a user who tested 1000+ strategies but found nothing profitable after fees and slippage.

## Background

Backtesting runs a strategy on past data. Overfitting and ignoring costs make results look better than they are. Ensembles of weak signals can be more robust. This is educational, not advice.

## What I took away

- Test on many instruments, not one.
- Keep strategies simple and account for costs.
- Combine weak signals and think about exits.
