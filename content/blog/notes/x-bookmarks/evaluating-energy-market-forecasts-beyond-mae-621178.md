## Summary

The author has worked in the energy market for a while and enjoys papers like the one shared. They say evaluation often comes down to simple MAE, and from experience the score correlates rarely strongly positively with actual results. The text is cut off.

## Background

MAE (mean absolute error) is the average size of forecast errors. It treats all errors the same, while in energy markets some errors cost much more than others. So a model with a better MAE may not earn or save more money.

## What I took away

- Pick evaluation metrics that match real business cost.
- A better error score does not guarantee better real outcomes.
- Test forecasts on the actual decision they support.
