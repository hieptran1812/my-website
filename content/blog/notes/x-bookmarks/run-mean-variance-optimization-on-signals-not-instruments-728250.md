## Summary

The author says mean-variance optimization (MVO) does not fail; its inputs do. Instruments are noisy, but signals are not, since the work of pulling pattern from noise is already done. They advise running MVO on signals and offer a free article to people who retweet.

## Background

MVO picks portfolio weights from expected returns and covariances, and small input errors can produce extreme weights. A signal is a processed prediction derived from raw price data. The claim is the author's view and is educational, not advice.

## What I took away

- MVO is very sensitive to noisy inputs.
- The author's idea is to optimize over signals rather than raw instruments.
- Treat this as one opinion and test it before trusting it.
