## Summary

The author points out that large-scale auto-research was done long before it became popular. The quoted post says the team ran similar experiments last year at a similar scale, about 6000 runs with a frontier model of the time, and concluded that LLMs struggle to make novel algorithmic improvements work. They measured a best score of 40% gap recovered against a reference.

## Background

Auto-research means letting AI models propose and run their own research experiments, such as trying new training tricks. Gap recovered measures how much of the difference between a baseline and a strong human result the AI manages to close. Another bookmark in this batch reports a newer open experiment where best runs closed 82% of a gap, so results may be improving or differ by setup.

## What I took away

- Compare auto-research results carefully; tasks and baselines differ between studies.
- Novel algorithmic ideas remain harder for LLMs than tuning known ones.
- Prior work often exists before a topic becomes popular; search for it.
