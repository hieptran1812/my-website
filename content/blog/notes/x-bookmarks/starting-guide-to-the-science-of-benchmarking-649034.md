## Summary

The author notes many evals are being shared and offers a short starting guide to the science of benchmarking. The goal is to help readers tell which numbers actually mean something.

## Background

Benchmarks measure model performance on fixed tasks, but results can mislead due to small test sets, noise between runs, data contamination or tuning to the test. Good benchmarking reports uncertainty and controls these factors.

## What I took away

- Ask for error bars or multiple runs before trusting small eval differences.
- Check for possible test-set contamination in training data.
- A single benchmark rarely captures real-world usefulness.
