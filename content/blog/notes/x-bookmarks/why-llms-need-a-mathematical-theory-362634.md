## Summary

The author quotes a claim that pretraining is still a mysterious dark art: no researcher knows what the model thinks during training, which is a problem if a run goes wrong. He says this is why we need a proper mathematical theory of LLMs, and that many such problems exist.

## Background

Pretraining is the large first phase of training on huge text data. It is expensive, and failures like loss spikes are often handled with heuristics. A theory would predict behavior instead of relying on trial and error.

## What I took away

- Much of large-scale training still depends on empirical rules of thumb.
- Theory could reduce the cost of failed training runs.
- Monitoring internals during training is an open problem.
