## Summary

Sander Dieleman says this is more evidence that 2026 is the year of continuous diffusion for language, and links his blog post on the topic. The quoted post introduces Sigma, described as the first large-scale (3B and 8B) continuous diffusion LM trained with likelihood, comparable to discrete diffusion LMs and autoregressive models on math and coding, and enabling study of inference-time interventions.

## Background

Discrete diffusion LMs corrupt and restore tokens directly, while continuous diffusion LMs work on continuous vectors (like image diffusion) and map them back to tokens. Continuous spaces may make guidance and editing during generation easier.

## What I took away

- Read Dieleman's blog for a clear overview of continuous diffusion for text.
- Inference-time interventions are a possible advantage over autoregressive models.
