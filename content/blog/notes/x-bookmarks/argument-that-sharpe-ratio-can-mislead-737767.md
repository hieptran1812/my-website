## Summary

@predict_addict argues that the Sharpe ratio, treated as the gold standard for risk-adjusted returns, can mislead in the real world. It can reward the wrong behavior and push portfolios toward outcomes that look optimal on paper but fail in practice.

## Background

Sharpe divides excess return by volatility. It treats upside and downside swings the same and assumes returns are roughly normal. Strategies that sell rare crashes can have a great Sharpe until a bad event hits.

## What I took away

- Sharpe ignores tail risk and skew.
- Look at drawdowns and other metrics alongside it.
- A high Sharpe on paper may not hold up out of sample.
