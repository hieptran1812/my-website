## Summary

Neel Nanda notes that one chain of thought is not enough since outputs branch widely. His scholars show early results. The quoted post says asking the same question 1000 times gives 1000 different chains, and they expose shared structure by clustering sentences and detecting recurring algorithmic steps.

## Background

Chains of thought differ every sample, so studying a single one can mislead. Aggregating many reveals common patterns.

## What I took away

- Study distributions of reasoning, not single samples.
- Clustering can expose structure.
