## Summary

The author argues continual learning is not impossible, as closed labs suggest; it simply conflicts with their business model, since local custom models compete with them. He credits Thinking Machines and Behrouz Ali. The quoted post introduces a phase, framed as LLMs needing sleep and dreaming, where the model consolidates fragile short-term memories into stable long-term memories.

## Background

Continual learning means models keep learning after release. The sleep analogy comes from neuroscience, where sleep is thought to move memories into long-term storage. For LLMs, short-term memory could be context or fast weights, and long-term memory the main weights.

## What I took away

- Separating fast short-term memory from slow consolidation is a useful design idea.
- The business-model argument is the author's opinion, not a demonstrated fact.
- Another bookmark suggests weight updates can degrade in-context learning; keep both views in mind.
