## Summary

The author summarizes the paper **Betting on the Leverage Effect** by Doshi et al.: stocks that go down when their volatility goes up tend to have higher returns, this subsumes the predictive power of historical skewness, and it is unrelated to the company's financial leverage. The quoted post cites the paper's claim of annual premiums of 3.72% and 5.76% for the overall and upside correlations in portfolio sorts.

## Background

The leverage effect is the observed tendency for volatility to rise when prices fall. The paper measures this correlation per stock and finds that stocks with a more negative correlation earn more, which can be read as compensation for risk. Portfolio sorts rank stocks by a measure and compare top and bottom groups.

## What I took away

- A stock's link between returns and volatility changes may carry information about future returns.
- Academic premiums often shrink after costs and publication.
- This is a research finding to study, not a trading recommendation.
