## Summary

The quoted post claims the 80% winner is whoever sits closest to the sequencer and that priority fees only move the latency race somewhere less visible. @mattyklein_ disputes this: priority fees improve taker (IOC order) latency by 30 to 300ms, and all 21 Hyperliquid nodes are in AWS AP Northeast-1 with only 1 to 3ms between them, so he says the closeness claim was made up.

## Background

A sequencer orders transactions on a blockchain or exchange. A taker order removes liquidity from the order book; IOC (immediate-or-cancel) fills now or is cancelled. Priority fees pay to get processed earlier.

## What I took away

- Check infrastructure facts before accepting claims about latency advantages.
- Fee mechanisms can matter more than physical location when nodes are co-located.
- This is market-structure education, not trading advice.
