## Summary

The author says Bayes' theorem is probably the single most important thing a rational person can learn. They argue many loud disagreements come from not understanding Bayes or how human rationality works. The post is cut off while starting to explain where the theorem got its name.

## Background

Bayes' theorem is a rule for updating a belief when you see new evidence. You start with a prior probability, weigh how likely the evidence is under each possibility, and get a posterior probability. It is the base of much of statistics and machine learning.

## What I took away

- Think of beliefs as probabilities that change with evidence.
- Always consider the prior (base rate), not just the new evidence.
- Many disputes are about different priors, not different facts.
