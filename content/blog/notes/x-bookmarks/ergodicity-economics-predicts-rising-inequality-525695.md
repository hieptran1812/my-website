## Summary

Ole Peters says people may like or dislike inequality, but economists should no longer be surprised by its rise, since their own models predict it by default when analyzed carefully. He cites his book An Introduction to Ergodicity Economics. The quoted post jokes that it is time to read up on the French Revolution.

## Background

Ergodicity economics looks at the difference between averages across many people and the growth one person experiences over time. With multiplicative random growth, like returns on wealth, these differ, and wealth tends to spread apart even when everyone faces the same odds.

## What I took away

- Under multiplicative growth, the average outcome can mislead about the typical one.
- Random returns alone can drive wealth inequality over time.
- This idea also matters for position sizing and risk.
