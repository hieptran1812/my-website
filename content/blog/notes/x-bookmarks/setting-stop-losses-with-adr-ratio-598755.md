## Summary

The author says most traders set arbitrary stops. Too tight and you get stopped out constantly, too wide and your winners make little. He says optimizing stops using the ADR ratio gives a balance of win rate and reward.

## Background

A stop loss is an order that exits a trade at a set loss. ADR is average daily range, how far a price typically moves in a day. Scaling stops to ADR ties them to the asset's normal volatility rather than a fixed number. This is education, not advice.

## What I took away

- Tie stop distance to typical volatility instead of guessing.
- Tight stops lower the win rate and wide stops shrink reward per win.
- Test any rule on data before trusting it.
