Most model families in this catalog are defined by a pretraining run. Qwen is whatever Alibaba pretrained, Llama is whatever Meta pretrained, and a new version number usually means a new base. Hermes is the exception that makes the category interesting. Nous Research has never shipped a Hermes model that it pretrained from scratch. Every Hermes, from the 13B Llama 1 fine-tune of June 2023 to the 36B model of late 2025, is a post-training recipe applied to someone else's checkpoint. The base has been a Llama, a Mistral, a Mixtral, a Yi, a SOLAR, a Qwen3 and a ByteDance Seed model. What stays constant is the recipe philosophy: synthetic instruction data at scale, a system prompt that the model actually obeys, structured tool use, and what Nous calls neutral alignment ("aligned to you", in the words of the Hermes 4.3 card).

That makes the family a useful lens on the whole open-weights ecosystem. If you want to know which base model the open community considered the best substrate at any point between 2023 and 2025, the Hermes release history is close to a running answer. When Mistral 7B came out, the next Hermes was on Mistral 7B. When Mixtral beat everything at its size, Hermes moved to Mixtral. When Llama 3.1 405B became the largest open dense model, Hermes 3 and Hermes 4 were trained on it. When Nous wanted an Apache-licensed mid-size model with a long context, it picked Seed-OSS-36B.

This post is a reference catalog of that arc, release by release, in the order Nous shipped them. For each generation you get the release window, the base model, what changed versus the previous generation (data, format, tooling, reasoning, training infrastructure, license), a table of the variants, and a primary-source link. It closes with the through-lines that connect the generations and a short guide to which Hermes still makes sense to run today. The figure below is the whole story at one glance; the rest of the post is the detail behind each mark on it.

A note on sourcing. Every date and specification here was checked against a Nous Research technical report on arXiv, a Nous blog post, or an official model card and `config.json` on Hugging Face (under the `NousResearch` or `teknium` organizations). Hugging Face gives a reliable repository creation date but not a public launch date, and the two can differ by days or weeks, so where only the repository date is known the tables say "repo created". Where a number is not in a primary source, the tables say so rather than guess. There is no Hermes 4.5 and no Hermes 5 as of 14 October 2026, so neither appears here.

## The timeline at a glance

![Release timeline of the Nous Research Hermes family from Nous-Hermes 13B in June 2023 to Hermes 4.3 36B in late 2025, with the base model under each release](/imgs/blogs/nous-hermes-model-family-timeline-1.webp)

Read the figure left to right and three phases stand out.

The first phase, from June 2023 to February 2024, is the **GPT-4 distillation era**. Hermes is a supervised fine-tune on a few hundred thousand to about a million synthetic instructions, mostly generated by GPT-4, and the interesting decisions are about data mix and chat format. The base model hops every few months: Llama 1, Llama 2, Mistral 7B, Yi-34B, Mixtral 8x7B, SOLAR 10.7B. This is also the era when OpenHermes 2.5 became one of the most widely reused open instruction datasets in the community.

The second phase, from March 2024 to early 2025, is the **capability era**. The recipe stops being "distill GPT-4 better" and starts adding specific capabilities: function calling and JSON mode in Hermes 2 Pro, model merging in Hermes 2 Theta, agentic tags and 128K context in Hermes 3, and finally a reasoning mode you can toggle with a system prompt in DeepHermes 3. The base consolidates on Llama 3 and Llama 3.1, with one Mistral Small detour.

The third phase, from mid-2025 on, is the **infrastructure era**. Hermes 4 is built on two in-house systems, the DataForge synthetic data generator and the Atropos reinforcement-learning environment framework, and its data is about fifty times larger than Hermes 3's. Hermes 4.3 is the first production Hermes post-trained on Psyche, Nous's decentralized training network. The base diversifies again: Llama 3.1 at the top, Qwen3 14B at the bottom, Seed-OSS-36B in the middle.

## Before Hermes: who Nous Research is

Nous Research started in 2023 as a loose collective of open-source fine-tuners rather than a company with a pretraining cluster. The early model cards credit individual contributors (Teknium, Karan4D and Emozilla lead most of them) and name the organization that donated compute for each run: Redmond AI for the first Llama 1 and Llama 2 models, PygmalionAI for the Llama 2 70B. That origin explains the shape of the family. A group without its own pretraining budget competes on post-training, and post-training is portable across bases. When a better base appears, you rerun the recipe.

It also explains why Hermes models were, for most of their history, released in many sizes across many bases at once rather than as one flagship. The unit of work was the dataset and the recipe; the models were the dataset applied to whatever good checkpoint existed that month. Later, as Nous raised funding and built infrastructure (the DisTrO optimizer, the Psyche network, the Atropos RL framework), the family shifted from "one recipe on many bases" to "one large recipe on a few carefully chosen bases", but the post-training-only identity never changed.

One terminology note. The early releases carry three different prefixes: **Nous-Hermes** (Nous's own releases), **OpenHermes** (Teknium's releases under the `teknium` Hugging Face account, with fully public data), and later plain **Hermes**. They are one lineage. OpenHermes 2.5 is literally the base of Nous-Hermes-2-Mistral-7B-DPO, and its dataset is the backbone of Hermes 2 Pro.

## 1. Nous-Hermes 13B on Llama 1 (June 2023)

The first Hermes is a full fine-tune of Llama 1 13B, released on Hugging Face as `NousResearch/Nous-Hermes-13b` (repo created 3 June 2023). The model card describes the training data as "over 300,000 instructions", almost entirely synthetic GPT-4 outputs, drawn from a mix of community datasets and two Nous-internal sets: GPTeacher, Nous Instruct, PDACTL (the last two unpublished), CodeAlpaca, Evol-Instruct, GPT4-LLM, Unnatural Instructions, Camel-AI and Airoboros. Teknium and Karan4D led the fine-tune; Redmond AI sponsored the compute.

What made it notable at the time was the combination of a large, clean synthetic mix and long, detailed responses with a low refusal rate, which is a theme that never leaves the family. The prompt format was Alpaca-style (`### Instruction:` / `### Response:`), the context window was Llama 1's 2,048 tokens, and the license on the card was GPL, an odd choice that became moot when Llama 2 arrived a few weeks later.

Primary source: [Nous-Hermes-13b model card](https://huggingface.co/NousResearch/Nous-Hermes-13b).

A code-oriented side release, Redmond-Hermes-Coder (repo created 28 June 2023), shows the code-specialist instinct was present from the first month, but nothing later builds on it, so the rest of this post ignores it.

## 2. Nous-Hermes on Llama 2 (July to August 2023)

Meta released Llama 2 on 18 July 2023 with a commercial-use license and a 4K context. Nous had the Hermes recipe running on it within days: `Nous-Hermes-Llama2-13b` (repo created 20 July 2023) and `Nous-Hermes-llama-2-7b` (25 July 2023), followed a month later by `Nous-Hermes-Llama2-70b` (22 August 2023). If you want the base-model side of this story, the [Llama family timeline](/blog/machine-learning/frontier-models/llama-model-family-timeline) covers what changed between Llama 1 and Llama 2.

What changed versus the first Hermes is almost entirely the base. The cards state that the 7B and 13B were trained on the same dataset as the Llama 1 model, with Teknium and Emozilla leading and Redmond AI again on compute. The context doubles to 4,096 tokens because Llama 2's does. The 70B is the first Hermes at that scale; its card credits PygmalionAI for compute and records the run as 4,096-token sequences on eight H100 80GB GPUs. The card license switched to MIT, though in practice the Llama 2 Community License of the base governs use.

The lesson of this generation is the one the family keeps relearning: a fine-tune is capped by its base. The 13B on Llama 2 was better than the 13B on Llama 1 for reasons that had nothing to do with Nous. That is why the team moved so quickly whenever a stronger base appeared.

## 3. OpenHermes on Llama 2 (September 2023)

OpenHermes is Teknium's answer to a fair criticism of the first two generations: the datasets were partly private, so nobody could reproduce the models. `teknium/OpenHermes-13B` (repo created 6 September 2023) and `teknium/OpenHermes-7B` (14 September 2023) are trained on 242,000 entries, mostly GPT-4 generated, using the same public sources as Nous-Hermes but with the private Nous Instruct and PDACTL sets removed. The dataset itself was published at the same time as [`teknium/openhermes`](https://huggingface.co/datasets/teknium/openhermes) (repo created 4 September 2023).

So the change versus Nous-Hermes Llama 2 is not capability but provenance. The base is still Llama 2, the context is still 4,096, the format is still Alpaca, and the dataset is slightly smaller because the private parts are gone. What you gain is that every token the model saw in fine-tuning is downloadable. For a community that was building its own fine-tunes, that mattered more than a point or two on a benchmark.

A small side release from the same week, Phi-Hermes-1.3B (repo created 13 September 2023), applied the recipe to a 1.3B Phi model; it is a curiosity rather than a generation.

## 4. OpenHermes 2 and 2.5 on Mistral 7B (October 2023)

Mistral 7B landed on 27 September 2023 and, for a while, beat Llama 2 13B on most benchmarks at about half the size. Two weeks later Teknium released `teknium/OpenHermes-2-Mistral-7B` (repo created 12 October 2023), and two weeks after that `teknium/OpenHermes-2.5-Mistral-7B` (29 October 2023). This pair is arguably the most influential Hermes release, not because of the model but because of what it fixed as conventions.

Three things changed versus OpenHermes 1:

1. **The base moved off Llama** for the first time, to Mistral 7B v0.1, with its 32K position limit (`max_position_embeddings` 32,768 in the config) and an Apache-2.0 license that removed the Llama license question entirely.
2. **The format moved to ChatML.** The OpenHermes 2 card describes converting the data to ShareGPT form and then to ChatML through axolotl, with real system prompts. ChatML (`<|im_start|>system ... <|im_end|>`) is the format every later Hermes uses, and the decision to train on system prompts is what makes later Hermes models unusually steerable. If you have ever lost a day to a model that ignores its chat template, the [chat-template bug catalog](/blog/machine-learning/debugging-training/chat-template-and-formatting-bugs) is the companion read.
3. **The data grew to 900k entries (OpenHermes 2) and then 1M (OpenHermes 2.5).** The 2.5 card describes the extra data as code, at roughly 7 to 14% of the mix, and reports HumanEval pass@1 rising from 43% to 50.7%. It also notes that adding code improved several non-code benchmarks, an early public data point for "code data helps reasoning" that later reports would make into a standard ingredient.

The OpenHermes-2.5 dataset had a long second life. It shows up in countless community fine-tunes as a general-purpose SFT mix, it is the kind of corpus the [distillation post](/blog/machine-learning/large-language-model/distillation-in-llm) means by "the SFT-via-GPT-4 ecosystem", and it is the canonical example of the padding problem in [sequence packing for fine-tuning](/blog/machine-learning/training-techniques/sequence-packing-llm-fine-tuning): short, variable-length chat samples that waste most of a fixed-length batch unless you pack them.

## 5. Nous Hermes 2 across many bases (November 2023 to February 2024)

Nous Hermes 2 is less a single model than a dataset generation applied to every strong base that existed that winter. The cards describe roughly 1,000,000 entries of primarily GPT-4 generated data plus other high-quality open data, all in ChatML. Six releases matter:

- **Nous-Hermes-2-Vision-Alpha** (repo created 28 November 2023): a LLaVA-style vision model on Mistral 7B with a SigLIP-400M image encoder. Its card also introduces a function-calling format (`<fn_call>`), the first sign of what Hermes 2 Pro would formalize. It stayed an alpha; vision did not become a Hermes line.
- **Nous-Hermes-2-Yi-34B** (23 December 2023): on 01.AI's Yi-34B, the strongest open model in the 30B class at the time, with a 4,096 context in the config.
- **Nous-Hermes-2-Mixtral-8x7B-SFT** (26 December 2023) and **-DPO** (11 January 2024): on Mistral's Mixtral 8x7B mixture of experts. The card claims this was the first model to beat Mistral's own Mixtral Instruct finetune on their benchmark set. The DPO variant is the first Hermes with a preference-optimization stage; if DPO is new to you, the [DPO deep-dive](/blog/machine-learning/reinforcement-learning/dpo-direct-preference-optimization) covers the method.
- **Nous-Hermes-2-SOLAR-10.7B** (1 January 2024): on Upstage's depth-upscaled SOLAR 10.7B, 4,096 context.
- **Nous-Hermes-2-Llama-2-70B** (23 January 2024): the Hermes 2 data on Llama 2 70B, 4,096 context.
- **Nous-Hermes-2-Mistral-7B-DPO** (18 February 2024): a DPO pass on top of OpenHermes-2.5-Mistral-7B, whose card points to the OpenHermes-2.5 dataset as the SFT source. This is the cleanest evidence that OpenHermes and Nous-Hermes are one lineage.

What changed versus OpenHermes 2.5: the data generation is the same scale, but the recipe now includes DPO on some variants, the vision and function-calling experiments appear, and for the first time Hermes ships on a mixture-of-experts base (Mixtral, 46.7B total with about 12.9B active per token according to Mistral's announcement). The defining trait of the family is at its most visible here: five different base architectures in about ten weeks.

## 6. Hermes 2 Pro: function calling and JSON mode (March to June 2024)

Hermes 2 Pro is the generation where Hermes stops being only a better chat model and becomes a tool-use model. `NousResearch/Hermes-2-Pro-Mistral-7B` (repo created 11 March 2024) is trained on a cleaned version of the OpenHermes 2.5 data plus an in-house function-calling and JSON-mode dataset. The card reports 90% on a function-calling evaluation built with Fireworks.AI and 84% on a structured JSON-output evaluation.

What changed versus Nous Hermes 2:

- **A `tool` role in ChatML.** Tool results come back to the model in their own turn rather than being stuffed into a user message, which makes multi-turn tool use parseable.
- **An XML-tagged calling convention.** The system prompt lists function signatures inside `<tools></tools>`, the model emits calls inside `<tool_call></tool_call>`, and results come back inside `<tool_response></tool_response>`. On the Llama 3 8B version these tags are single tokens in the tokenizer, which makes them cheap to emit and easy to stream-parse.
- **Reference code.** Nous published the prompt templates and a parser in [NousResearch/Hermes-Function-Calling](https://github.com/NousResearch/Hermes-Function-Calling).

This convention outlived the model. The `<tool_call>{"name": ..., "arguments": {...}}</tool_call>` shape became what the [tool-calling fine-tuning guide](/blog/machine-learning/training-techniques/fine-tuning-tool-calling-llms-when-how) calls the "Hermes-style" template, and it is the format several later open model families and serving engines adopted as a default tool parser.

Llama 3 arrived on 18 April 2024 and Hermes 2 Pro followed it: `Hermes-2-Pro-Llama-3-8B` (30 April 2024) and `Hermes-2-Pro-Llama-3-70B` (25 June 2024), both at Llama 3's 8,192-token context.

## 7. Hermes 2 Theta: the merge experiment (May to June 2024)

Hermes 2 Theta is the one generation built partly by merging rather than training. `Hermes-2-Theta-Llama-3-8B` (repo created 5 May 2024) was made with Charles Goddard of Arcee AI, the author of MergeKit: Hermes 2 Pro Llama 3 8B was merged with Meta's Llama 3 8B Instruct, and the merged model then received a further RLHF stage. `Hermes-2-Theta-Llama-3-70B` (14 June 2024) repeated the approach at 70B and added a feature the card describes as extracting structured features from retrieved documents.

The idea is straightforward. Meta's Instruct model carries a large, expensive preference-tuning run that Nous could not reproduce, while Hermes 2 Pro carries the tool-use and steerability recipe. Merging the weights tries to get both, and the follow-up RLHF repairs the rough edges the merge introduces. It is a legitimate technique, and it shows up in Hermes 3's report as context, but Theta is the only Hermes generation that leaned on it. From Hermes 3 on, Nous went back to training from a base model.

## 8. Hermes 3 on Llama 3.1 (August 2024)

Hermes 3 is the first Hermes with a technical report: [Hermes 3 Technical Report](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.11857), submitted to arXiv on 15 August 2024. It shipped on Llama 3.1 at 8B, 70B and 405B (repositories created 28 July, 29 July and 13 August 2024), and the report claims state-of-the-art results among open-weight models for the 405B on several public benchmarks. A 3B model on Llama 3.2 followed in December 2024 (`Hermes-3-Llama-3.2-3B`, repo created 3 December 2024, trained on Lambda H100s according to its card).

What changed versus Hermes 2 Pro and Theta:

- **Context jumps to 128K**, inherited from Llama 3.1 (`max_position_embeddings` 131,072). The fine-tune itself was packed at 8,192 tokens; the report states 96% packing efficiency.
- **The recipe is two stages, SFT then DPO.** The report describes four epochs of supervised fine-tuning with a modified Axolotl on six HGX nodes (eight H100s each) for the 8B and 70B, followed by DPO applied as a LoRA adapter rather than a full-weight update.
- **Data**: the report describes roughly 390M tokens, of which about 270M are response tokens that contribute to the loss. The Hermes 4 model cards describe Hermes 3's corpus as "1M samples and 1.2B tokens"; the two figures appear to count different things (the report's number is the curated SFT mixture), so this post uses the report's.
- **Agentic and reasoning tags.** The data teaches the model to use structured scratch spaces such as `<SCRATCHPAD>`, `<THINKING>` and `<PLAN>`, alongside the Hermes 2 Pro tool-calling format, retrieval citation, and stronger role-play and long-form writing.
- **An explicit alignment stance.** The card's ethos line is that Hermes aligns the model "to the user, with powerful steering capabilities and control given to the end user". In practice that means a strong system-prompt prior and a low refusal rate, which is the trait the family is best known for.

Hermes 3 is also the generation where the Hermes prompt format becomes something you can rely on across sizes. Here is the canonical Hermes 3 tool-calling exchange, which works unchanged on Hermes 2 Pro, Hermes 3 and Hermes 4, and which most open-source serving stacks can parse with a "hermes" tool parser:

```python
# pip install transformers==4.46.3
import json
from transformers import AutoTokenizer

tok = AutoTokenizer.from_pretrained("NousResearch/Hermes-3-Llama-3.1-8B")

tools = [{
    "type": "function",
    "function": {
        "name": "get_fx_rate",
        "description": "Spot FX rate between two ISO currency codes.",
        "parameters": {
            "type": "object",
            "properties": {
                "base": {"type": "string"},
                "quote": {"type": "string"},
            },
            "required": ["base", "quote"],
        },
    },
}]

system = (
    "You are a function calling AI model. You are provided with function "
    "signatures within <tools></tools> XML tags. You may call one or more "
    "functions to assist with the user query. Don't make assumptions about "
    "what values to plug into functions. Here are the available tools: "
    f"<tools> {json.dumps(tools)} </tools> "
    "For each function call return a json object with function name and "
    "arguments within <tool_call></tool_call> XML tags."
)

messages = [
    {"role": "system", "content": system},
    {"role": "user", "content": "How many yen is one US dollar right now?"},
]

# ChatML with a generation prompt; the model should answer with
# <tool_call>{"name": "get_fx_rate", "arguments": {"base": "USD", "quote": "JPY"}}</tool_call>
prompt = tok.apply_chat_template(messages, tokenize=False, add_generation_prompt=True)
print(prompt)

# After you run the tool, return its output in a `tool` turn:
messages.append({"role": "assistant", "content":
    '<tool_call>{"name": "get_fx_rate", "arguments": {"base": "USD", "quote": "JPY"}}</tool_call>'})
messages.append({"role": "tool", "content":
    '<tool_response>{"rate": 151.2}</tool_response>'})
print(tok.apply_chat_template(messages, tokenize=False, add_generation_prompt=True))
```

The system prompt text above is the one printed on the Hermes 3 model card. The FX rate in the example is a placeholder tool result, not a real quote.

## 9. DeepHermes 3 Preview: reasoning as a switch (February to March 2025)

DeepSeek R1 shipped in January 2025 and made long chain-of-thought reasoning the thing every open lab had to answer. Nous answered within weeks with **DeepHermes 3 Preview**: `DeepHermes-3-Llama-3-8B-Preview` (repo created 12 February 2025), `DeepHermes-3-Llama-3-3B-Preview` (16 February 2025) and `DeepHermes-3-Mistral-24B-Preview` (2 March 2025, on Mistral Small 24B).

The design choice that defines this generation is **one model, two modes**. A normal system prompt gives you a normal Hermes 3 style assistant: short answers, tool calls, role-play. A specific "deep thinking" system prompt switches on long reasoning inside `<think></think>` tags before the answer. The reasoning behaviour was distilled from DeepSeek R1 outputs (the [R1 post](/blog/machine-learning/frontier-models/deepseek-r1-incentivizing-reasoning-capability-in-llms-via-reinforcement-learning) explains where those traces came from). Most labs at the time shipped separate reasoning and non-reasoning checkpoints; putting both behaviours in one set of weights, gated by the system prompt, is the "hybrid reasoning" idea that Hermes 4 then scales up.

The Llama-based previews are named "Llama-3" but their configs carry Llama 3.1-style RoPE scaling and a 131,072 position limit, so the 8B is on Llama 3.1 8B and the 3B on Llama 3.2 3B.

### The Atropos specialists (April 2025)

In late April 2025 (repository created 29 April 2025) Nous released [Atropos](https://github.com/NousResearch/atropos), an open framework of reinforcement-learning environments, together with a handful of small RL experiments trained with it: `DeepHermes-ToolCalling-Specialist-Atropos`, the `Egregore` RLAIF models, `AscensionMaze-RLAIF-8b` and a financial-fundamentals prediction specialist. These are not a Hermes generation; they are proofs that the environment framework works. They matter here because Atropos is the verification layer of the Hermes 4 data pipeline four months later.

## 10. Hermes 4: hybrid reasoning at scale (August 2025)

Hermes 4 is the largest change in the family's history, documented in the [Hermes 4 Technical Report](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.18255) (arXiv submission 25 August 2025, revised 2 September 2025). It ships at three sizes: 405B and 70B on Llama 3.1, and 14B on Qwen3 14B, the first time the family's top line used a non-Llama base at the small end. The repositories were created on 6 August (405B), 18 August (70B) and 30 August 2025 (14B); FP8 versions of the large models were published alongside. The exact public launch day is not stated in the sources I could verify, but the report's submission date puts it in the last week of August 2025.

What changed versus Hermes 3 and DeepHermes 3:

- **Hybrid reasoning by default.** Every Hermes 4 model can answer directly or reason first inside `<think>` tags, and the report trains with strict enforcement of the think delimiters at the start of the assistant turn.
- **Data scale.** About 5 million samples and 19 billion tokens: 3.5 million reasoning samples and 1.6 million non-reasoning samples, with reasoning traces up to about 16K tokens. Compared with the roughly 390M-token Hermes 3 SFT mixture, that is about fifty times more data. (The model cards say "~5M samples / ~60B tokens"; the report's 19B is the dataset, and its separate per-model figure of 56B is tokens trained, which includes multiple passes. This post cites the report.)
- **DataForge.** A graph-based synthetic data generator the report describes as inspired by AgentInstruct: each training sample is produced by a random walk through a directed acyclic graph of transformation nodes, so one seed document can become many structurally different tasks.
- **Rejection sampling against Atropos verifiers.** Candidate responses are kept only if they pass one of roughly a thousand task-specific verifiers implemented as Atropos environments. This is the RL infrastructure from April used as a data filter rather than as an online RL loop.
- **New training stack.** A modified TorchTitan on 192 NVIDIA B200 GPUs, 16,384-token sequences, a global batch of 384, 9,000 steps, and 56B tokens trained per model. Samples were packed with First-Fit Decreasing at above 99.9% efficiency, with Flex Attention masking so packed samples do not attend to each other.
- **Length control.** The 14B model tended to run its reasoning past its 40,960-token window. A second SFT stage teaches it to emit `</think>` once a 30,000-token budget is reached, training only on the termination decision. The report notes that a stricter version of this control cost about 20 points on AIME'24, which is the clearest public number I know of for the price of truncating reasoning.
- **RefusalBench.** A new internal benchmark of 166 prompts across 32 categories that scores how often a model answers rather than refuses, which is the alignment stance of the family turned into a metric.

### What the B200 hours tell you

The training-cost column is worth a second look, because it is unusually honest data. All three models trained on the same 56B tokens, so the hours scale with model size. The 405B is about 29 times the parameters of the 14B and took about 16 times the GPU hours (71,616 versus 4,454), and the 70B is 5 times the parameters for about 2.9 times the hours. Large models use the hardware more efficiently per parameter, which is the usual result. At a rough cloud price of three dollars per B200 hour (an assumption for illustration, not a Nous figure), the 405B post-training run is on the order of 215,000 dollars of compute and the 14B about 13,000 dollars. That is the economic core of the Hermes model: a frontier-scale post-train costs two to three orders of magnitude less than the pretraining run it sits on.

## 11. Hermes 4.3 36B: post-trained on Psyche (November to December 2025)

Hermes 4.3 is a single model, `NousResearch/Hermes-4.3-36B` (repo created 17 November 2025), announced in the Nous blog post [Introducing Hermes 4.3](https://nousresearch.com/introducing-hermes-4-3) (page metadata dated 1 December 2025; the exact public launch day between those two dates is not something I could pin down). Its base is ByteDance's `Seed-OSS-36B-Base`, a dense 36B model with grouped-query attention (80 query heads, 8 KV heads, 64 layers in the config) and a native 512K context; the [Seed-OSS-36B post](/blog/machine-learning/frontier-models/seed-oss-36b-open-long-context-thinking-budget) covers that base in depth.

What changed versus Hermes 4:

- **Where the compute lives.** Hermes 4.3 is the first production Hermes post-trained entirely on Psyche, Nous's decentralized training network, using the DisTrO optimizer. The blog reports an average throughput of about 144,000 tokens per second across 24 Psyche nodes, for a run it describes as twice the size of the Hermes 4 post-train.
- **A controlled comparison.** Nous also published `Hermes-4.3-36B-centralized` (repo created 28 October 2025), the same recipe trained on its centralized TorchTitan fork, as a research artifact. Having both lets anyone compare a decentralized and a centralized post-train of the same model directly, which is rarer than it should be.
- **Context and license.** The config carries 524,288 positions and the card says it was trained "up to 512K"; the license is Apache-2.0, inherited from Seed-OSS. That makes 4.3 the first Hermes that combines a permissive license, long context and a mid-size footprint.
- **Results as claimed.** The blog says Hermes 4.3 36B nearly matches Hermes 4 70B at about half the parameters and is state of the art on RefusalBench. I have not seen independent evaluations, so treat both as the vendor's claims.

## How the family evolved

![What each Hermes generation added to the recipe, from 300k GPT-4 instructions in 2023 to decentralized post-training on Psyche in 2025](/imgs/blogs/nous-hermes-model-family-timeline-2.webp)

Step back from the individual releases and five through-lines account for almost everything.

### 1. The base model is a variable, not an identity

Twelve base models from six labs (Meta, Mistral AI, 01.AI, Upstage, Alibaba's Qwen team and ByteDance Seed) in two and a half years. No other family in this catalog looks like that. The practical consequence is that "how good is Hermes" is always two questions: how good is the base, and how much does the recipe add. Hermes 4 70B and Hermes 4 14B are from the same recipe and the same 56B training tokens, but they inherit very different knowledge, tokenizers, licenses and context windows from Llama 3.1 and Qwen3. When you compare a Hermes against something else, compare it against its own base first.

The license column follows the same rule. Every Llama-based Hermes is bound by the Llama license regardless of what the Hermes card says; the Mistral, Mixtral, SOLAR, Qwen3 and Seed-OSS based releases are Apache-2.0. Two cards (the early MIT tags and the Theta 8B's Apache-2.0) are misleading on this point, which is why the tables note the base license.

### 2. Data went from distilled to synthesized to verified

The data story has three steps. In 2023 the data was GPT-4 distillation: a few hundred thousand to a million prompts answered by a stronger closed model. In 2024 it became targeted synthesis: in-house function-calling and JSON datasets, agentic tags, a DPO pass. In 2025 it became a pipeline: DataForge generates tasks by walking a graph, a thousand Atropos verifiers reject the bad samples, and reasoning traces are kept only if they check out. The scale grew with each step, from about 300k instructions to about 5M samples and 19B tokens, but the more important change is that the quality signal moved from "the teacher said it" to "a verifier confirmed it". That is the same shift the rest of the field made from SFT-via-GPT-4 to RL with verifiable rewards, done here as a data filter.

### 3. Format conventions outlived the models

ChatML with real system prompts (OpenHermes 2, October 2023) and the `<tools>` / `<tool_call>` / `<tool_response>` convention with a `tool` role (Hermes 2 Pro, March 2024) are probably the family's most durable contribution. Most people who use the Hermes tool format today have never run a Hermes model; they are using a serving engine's "hermes" tool parser or a template that copied the shape. If you are building tool-use training data, the format question is covered in the [tool-calling fine-tuning guide](/blog/machine-learning/training-techniques/fine-tuning-tool-calling-llms-when-how).

### 4. Reasoning arrived as a switch, not a separate model

DeepHermes 3 (February 2025) put reasoning and non-reasoning behaviour in one set of weights, gated by a system prompt; Hermes 4 made that the default and added a learned budget to stop runaway traces. The AIME'24 gap on Hermes 4 405B (81.9 reasoning versus 11.4 direct) shows why that design is worth the trouble: one deployment can serve both a chat product and a solver. The 30K-token budget experiment shows the cost side. Cutting reasoning short is not free, and a too-strict budget cost about 20 points on AIME'24.

### 5. The compute moved from donated GPUs to a network

The first Hermes was trained on GPUs a sponsor lent for the run. Hermes 3 trained on six rented HGX nodes, Hermes 4 on 192 B200s with a TorchTitan fork, and Hermes 4.3 on Psyche, a network of independent nodes coordinated over the internet with DisTrO. For the field, the interesting part is the paired centralized and decentralized 4.3 checkpoints, which turn "can you post-train a useful model over the open internet" from a claim into something you can measure.

## Which variant to pick today

![Decision tree for choosing a Hermes model by reasoning need, hardware and license](/imgs/blogs/nous-hermes-model-family-timeline-3.webp)

The figure is the short version. The longer version, as of October 2026:

| Your situation | Pick | Why | Watch out for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Laptop, phone or a small single GPU, no long reasoning | Hermes 3 Llama 3.2 3B or Hermes 3 Llama 3.1 8B | Mature, widely quantized, reliable Hermes tool format, 128K context | Llama license; weak at multi-step math |
| General assistant on one node, no long reasoning | Hermes 3 70B | Strong system-prompt following and tool use without reasoning-token overhead | Older knowledge cutoff; Hermes 4 70B in direct mode is the newer option |
| Reasoning on a single GPU | Hermes 4 14B | Hybrid reasoning, Apache-2.0, Qwen3 base | 40,960-token window fills fast in reasoning mode; set a budget |
| Reasoning plus long context or a permissive license at mid size | Hermes 4.3 36B | Seed-OSS base, 512K context, Apache-2.0, claimed near Hermes 4 70B quality | Vendor-reported quality; 36B dense needs about 72 GB in BF16 |
| Maximum quality, multi-node, Llama license acceptable | Hermes 4 70B or 405B | Best open Hermes scores, documented in the report | 405B dense is expensive to serve; use the FP8 release |
| Low refusal rate is a requirement | Any Hermes 4 or 4.3 | RefusalBench is the family's design target | You own the guardrails; add a moderation layer if the product needs one |

Two pieces of general advice apply across the table. First, match the chat template exactly: every modern Hermes is ChatML with a system turn, and most "Hermes is worse than its base" reports are template or tool-parser mismatches rather than model problems. Second, decide the reasoning mode per request, not per deployment. The hybrid models are only cheap if you leave reasoning off for the requests that do not need it.

What not to pick in 2026: anything before Hermes 2 Pro, unless you are reproducing a 2023 paper. Nous-Hermes on Llama 1 and 2, OpenHermes, and the Hermes 2 models on Yi, SOLAR and Mixtral are historical artifacts now; their bases have been surpassed several times over, and later Hermes versions carry the same recipe on much better substrates. The one 2023 artifact still worth downloading is the OpenHermes-2.5 dataset, which remains a reasonable general SFT mix to blend into a fine-tune.
