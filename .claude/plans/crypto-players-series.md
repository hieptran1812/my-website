# Crypto Players & Power Structure — Blog Series Plan

**Subcategory:** `trading/crypto-players/`
**Display label:** "Crypto Players"
**Size:** 48 posts, 8 waves
**Angle:** The definitive, plumbing-level map of **who actually moves crypto prices** — the venture funds (a16z, Paradigm, Polychain, Multicoin, Pantera, DCG), the market makers (Wintermute, Jump, DWF Labs, GSR, Cumberland, Alameda), the exchanges-as-players (Binance, Coinbase), the token foundations & treasuries, and the OGs/whales/KOLs — plus the **operating model of each** and the **exact mechanics by which their actions show up in price**. Every post moves **who they are → how they make money → how that touches the order book → a named, sourced case study → what it means if you're on the other side of the trade**. Not conspiracy, not shilling — the machinery, built from zero, with real dollar arithmetic and fact-checked figures.
**Depth:** all `deep-dive` (`featured: true`). Each post: an operating-model/mechanism section, ≥1 named real case study, ≥1 worked numeric walkthrough (spread capture, unlock overhang, FDV vs float, MM option payoff, OTC block), a "how it shows up in price" section, and a defensive takeaway for retail. Intuitive Excalidraw figures; 1–3 animated where motion carries meaning (order-book fill, unlock cliff, capital rotation).
**Voice:** accessible-expert (finance-writer). Honest, opinionated, no fabrication. Sibling of the existing `crypto-vc-and-market-makers` post (which this series expands into a full track).
**Language:** English only (verify gate enforces). User briefed in Vietnamese; posts ship in English.

## WAVE 1 — The Map: Who Actually Moves Crypto (Foundations) (6)
1. **the-hidden-power-structure-of-crypto** — Master map of the whole series: the layers (VCs → market makers → exchanges → foundations → OGs/whales → KOLs → retail) and how influence flows down into the price you see. The reference figure everything else drills into.
2. **why-a-token-is-not-a-stock** — The structural gap that makes all of this possible: no IPO gate, no exchange-enforced lockup, no disclosure, and insiders who exit on a public market years before an equity investor could. Float vs FDV from zero.
3. **the-lifecycle-of-a-token-seed-to-unlock** — The pipeline every token walks — seed → private → strategic → TGE → listing → cliff → unlocks — and exactly where each player enters and at what price. The timeline figure.
4. **how-crypto-prices-actually-move** — Order books, resting liquidity, thin float, slippage, and reflexivity: the mechanics by which a *small* player can move a *large* headline number. Worked slippage example.
5. **follow-the-money-reading-a-tokens-cap-table** — How to read who owns what: tokenomics tables, allocation %, vesting schedules, and basic on-chain forensics (Etherscan/Arkham) to see insider wallets.
6. **cui-bono-the-incentive-map-of-crypto** — The structural conflict of interest across the stack: who profits at each step, why insiders and launch-day retail collide, and why "aligned incentives" marketing rarely survives the arithmetic.

## WAVE 2 — Venture Capital: The Kingmakers (6)
7. **the-crypto-vc-operating-model** — How a crypto fund actually works: LPs, fund structure, the liquid-vs-locked token book, management fees + carry, and the crypto-specific twist — a public exit years earlier than equity. The business model, with a worked return example.
8. **a16z-crypto-the-institutional-giant** — Profile: multi-billion fund sizes, the thesis, portfolio breadth, governance-token voting power, and the DC lobbying/policy machine (the "a16z playbook"). How its backing itself becomes a price signal.
9. **paradigm-and-the-research-driven-fund** — Profile: research + capital as one weapon (Uniswap, MEV research, technical standards); how a fund that *shapes the tech* also shapes the market.
10. **polychain-multicoin-and-the-thesis-funds** — Profiles: Polychain (Olaf Carlson-Wee) and Multicoin (Kyle Samani / Tushar Jain) — concentrated, high-conviction thesis bets (Solana, etc.), liquid + venture, and the volatility of that model.
11. **pantera-dcg-and-the-crypto-conglomerates** — Pantera (one of the earliest) and DCG/Grayscale/Genesis (Barry Silbert): the conglomerate model where a fund also owns an OTC desk, a trust, and a lender — and how that concentration became contagion.
12. **how-vcs-move-price-listings-unlocks-and-narrative** — The mechanics of VC price impact: getting a portfolio token listed, seeding the narrative, and the unlock overhang that transfers supply to the market on a *known* schedule. Composite worked example.

## WAVE 3 — Market Makers: The Liquidity Engine (Core Mechanics) (6)
13. **what-a-crypto-market-maker-actually-does** — The operating model from zero: two-sided quoting, the spread, inventory, adverse selection, and why a token literally cannot trade without one. Worked spread-capture example.
14. **the-loan-plus-options-deal-how-market-makers-get-paid** — The crypto-native MM contract: the project *lends* the MM tokens to quote with, and the MM keeps *call options* that pay off if price rises — so it profits from launching the very token it makes markets in. Worked option-payoff example. The single most important mechanic in the series.
15. **inventory-risk-hedging-and-delta-neutrality** — How MMs manage the tokens they're forced to hold: hedging spot inventory with perps/futures, staying delta-neutral, and why (usually) they don't want a directional bet. Worked hedge example.
16. **designated-versus-principal-market-making** — The two hats: exchange-designated liquidity provision vs. proprietary trading — and the sharp conflict when one firm wears both (it quotes your book *and* trades against the flow it sees).
17. **wash-trading-spoofing-and-manufactured-volume** — The dark side: fake volume to fake liquidity, how it's detected on-chain and by exchanges, exchange incentives to look away, and the reported DWF Labs allegations (framed as reported).
18. **cross-exchange-arbitrage-and-the-latency-game** — How MMs keep one price across many venues, the HFT-in-crypto angle (co-location, low-latency infra), and why arbitrage is both a service and a toll.

29. **launchpads-airdrops-and-the-points-meta** — How new supply is distributed (Launchpad, IDO, airdrops, "points" farming) and who actually captures the value versus who provides the exit liquidity.
