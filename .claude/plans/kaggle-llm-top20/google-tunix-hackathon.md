# Google Tunix Hack: Train a model to show its work — all 15 prize winners

- **Slug / URL:** `google-tunix-hackathon`, https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/google-tunix-hackathon
- **Dates:** 2025-11-11 to 2026-01-12 (deadline 2026-01-12 23:59 UTC). Winners announced 2026-04-15 in host thread https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/google-tunix-hackathon/discussion/691572 (by windmaple).
- **Entrants:** 319 teams, 444 competitors, 319 submissions (competition metadata). The Google Developers Blog recap (2026-05-28, https://developers.googleblog.com/how-the-community-trained-gemma-to-think-with-tunix-and-tpus/) says "over 11,000 entrants and 300+ submissions" (that figure presumably counts joiners, not submitters).
- **Prize pool:** $100,000. Main track 1st $30k, 2nd $25k, 3rd $15k, 4th to 6th $10k each; 9 Honorable Mentions (no cash in the tracks API).
- **Task:** use Tunix (JAX post-training library) to train Gemma 2 2B or Gemma 3 1B to emit `<reasoning>…</reasoning><answer>…</answer>`; Kaggle TPU (v5e-8), 9 h per session; output under 1K tokens; English; no tools.
- **Rubric:** Notebook quality 35; Model quality from a single 9 h Kaggle session 45 (LLM-as-judge plus human, held-out verifiable and non-verifiable prompts); Video 20; optional +15 for a multi-session / private-data model submitted as a Kaggle Model.
- **Bank coverage:** `kaggle-llm-writeup-bank-part4.md` H2 "## 4." covers 1st (G-RaR), 2nd (Pinocchio-1B), 3rd (IDEA-E) and 5th (OPD) in depth. This file does not repeat that depth; it adds 4th (SandSpec, recovered from a host-published notebook), 6th (TheItCrow) and all 9 Honorable Mentions in full, plus the extra matrix rows for everyone.

## Per-winner synthesis

### 1st: G-RaR (Dubai Chewy Cookie) — bank covers in depth
See bank H2 "## 4." for the recipe (Gemma-2-2B-IT, LoRA SFT then GRPO; reward = format + exact answer + G-RaR expected-score from a Gemma-3-12B judge over 3-tier rubrics pre-generated by Gemma-3-27B; 69,392 samples, HF `comoZ/reasoning-dataset`). New facts for the matrix: training and judging both run inside **one Kaggle TPU v5e-8** with a split **(1,4)/(1,4)** mesh, so the whole pipeline fits the single-session rule (writeup). The writeup's argument for SFT being "mandatory" is three-fold: without format, verification rewards stay near zero; unstructured outputs make policy-gradient estimates noisy; cold-start GRPO must learn format and reasoning at once and risks collapse (writeup). The Google Developers Blog recap repeats the split-mesh design as the headline idea.

### 2nd: Pinocchio-1B (Distilled Santa, David Babu) — bank covers in depth
Bank has the three acts (SFT distillation from gpt-oss-120B with Gemini task-type router and NoseCheck filter; SimPO in a patched `DPOTrainer`; GRPO with async Gemini 2.0 Flash judge). New facts: the subtitle states the full chain runs "within a single 9-hour TPU session"; the author wrote custom safetensors conversion scripts to chain SFT → SimPO → GRPO checkpoints inside one session; headline claim "15% accuracy improvement over the base model across 17 domains", 57% → ~72% on 1,000 held-out samples (writeup). The Developers Blog adds that SimPO was chosen over DPO partly to cut memory (no reference model).

### 3rd: IDEA-E (Kevin Power) — bank covers in depth
Bank has the 5-stage scaffold, 27,198 DeepSeek-Reasoner traces, TF-IDF-to-teacher reward and the 4-phase curriculum. New facts: the stated config is "for a single 9-hour Colab notebook run", training duration ~9 h on **TPU v6e-1** (Colab), LoRA 64/64, LR 1e-6, micro batch 1, 15,000 steps; ablations were run on a Colab single-chip TPU and the writeup notes they score lower than the final Kaggle eight-chip runs (writeup). The Developers Blog highlights the TF-IDF reward as a CPU-side, non-blocking substitute for a slow LLM judge.

### 4th: SandSpec (Sandeeplleb, Matthew Speciale) — "Tunix Hackathon"
**The writeup is 9 words** ("A pipeline that fine-tunes Tunix on how to reason."), yet the host cited "exceptional model quality and clear write-up". The 177-second video (channel MattyS737) has no description and its transcript could not be fetched (YouTube blocked the requests). The method below comes from the **host-published copy of their submission notebook**, [windmaple/mattys737-sandeeplleb-submission](https://www.kaggle.com/code/windmaple/mattys737-sandeeplleb-submission), which is presumably the judges' single-session re-run; the notebook's own markdown functions as the real write-up. The forum has no SandSpec solution post (their only comment in 691572 is about prize paperwork).

- **Model and hardware:** Gemma 2 2B IT (`google/gemma-2/flax/gemma2-2b-it`), Kaggle TPU v5e-8, LoRA rank 64 / alpha 64 on q/kv/attn-out and gate/up/down (via `qwix`).
- **Pipeline, SFT → DPO → GRPO (math) → GRPO (extra domains), all in one session.** Stated compute: SFT 22 min on ~3,000 records (3 epochs); DPO 16 min on ~5,800 pairs (1 epoch); GRPO-math 56 min on ~4,500 GSM8K; GRPO-extra 43 min on ~2,500. That is about 2 h 17 min of training, far under the 9 h cap (notebook).
- **SFT data:** a curated mix of public instruction sets (math incl. harder less-verifiable problems, multi-language coding, writing, summarization, puzzles, first-principles science, commonsense), all re-written by **Gemini-2.5-Flash** into the XML format, filtered for malformed and duplicate outputs; domain weights favour instruction following and creative writing. Dataset `vennasan/sft-gemini-gen-data`.
- **DPO data (the distinctive part):** aimed at non-verifiable domains. Round 1: responses from Gemma-2-2B-IT, Gemma-3-4B-IT and Gemini-2.0-Flash ranked by Gemini-2.5-Flash; chosen = well-formatted and high-ranked, rejected = bad format or low-ranked. Round 2: train an offline Gemma-2-2B-IT on round 1, generate on unseen prompts, harvest its failure cases (format and quality), add them as targeted pairs. Dataset `sandeeplleb/dpo-final`.
- **Hyperparameters:** SFT LR 2e-5, micro batch 2 × grad-accum 16 = 32, max length 2048, warmup 5% + cosine to 10%, weight decay 0.1, clip 0.1, mesh (8,1) FSDP. DPO β 0.2, LR 3e-6, effective batch 32, prompt/response 1024/1024. GRPO num_generations 4, β 0.08, ε 0.2, LR 3e-6, temperature 0.9, prompt 256 / generation 512 tokens, mesh (1,4) TP. The notebook explains the mesh choice per algorithm (FSDP for compute-bound SFT/DPO, TP for decode-heavy GRPO) and lists it as an ablation.
- **Rewards:** math stage = `match_format_exactly` 3.0 + `match_format_approximately` 2.0 + `check_answer` 3.0 (exact, substring or numeric-ratio partial credit) + `check_numbers` 1.5 (~9.5 max). Extra-domain stage = unified 1.0-point reward: format 0.4, accuracy 0.4, heuristic reasoning quality 0.1 (length, structure, flow, convergence), bonus 0.1. No LLM judge in the reward.
- **Evaluation:** a held-out "Universal test" set run greedily after every stage (pre-SFT, post-SFT, post-DPO, post-GRPO) for side-by-side comparison; **no quantitative score is reported** in the notebook.
- **Optional +15:** the multi-session model upload cell is commented out, so apparently single-session only.

### 5th: On-Policy Distillation (Shanai Nair) — bank covers in depth
Bank has teacher (Gemma 3 4B: SFT on 100k gpt-oss-120B traces → DPO → GRPO) and student (Gemma 3 1B: SFT → OPD). New facts: the teacher was trained offline and published as a Kaggle model; the writeup frames distillation as the most sample-efficient route under the 9 h single-session cap; OPD loss is PPO-style with token-level advantage normalisation; evaluation = LLM-judge ranking with win rates and mean ranks on **16k held-out prompts**, comparing 3 teacher checkpoints and SFT vs SFT+OPD; the reverse KL student→teacher converges to ~0.3 for the unrestricted model (writeup). Note: the Developers Blog recap files OPD (and Gemma2-Deep) under "Honorable Mentions" even though the host thread awards them 5th and 6th; the host thread is authoritative.

### 6th: TheItCrow (Kevin Bönisch) — Gemma2-Deep
Host reason: "creative reward design and well-made video". The writeup (self-described as a bit over 1,500 words, mostly tables) presents **pure GRPO on Gemma2-2b-it with LoRA**, a frozen gemma2-2b-it reference for the KL penalty, and **no SFT stage is described**; exact hyperparameters are deferred to the notebook (which could not be pulled: the API returned an empty body), so LoRA rank, LR, steps and TPU time are unknown.

- **Data, Deep-CoRGI:** 100k+ reasoning tasks distilled from the a-m-Team "1.4 Million Open-Source Distilled Reasoning Dataset", unified into `question / answer / thought / prompt` fields across math, reasoning, coding, writing and mixed. Sources include InfinityInstruct, OpenOrca, CodeIO, OpenCoder, MetaMathQA, NuminaMath-1.5, PRIME (14,834), natural_reasoning, OpenThoughts (2,967), OpenR1-Math, Omni-MATH, AIME (58), mostly capped at 10,000 each.
- **Process reward model, CoRGI-ThoughtTeacher (the distinctive idea):** the source traces have no preference signal, so the author creates a corrupted alternative per trace (deleted sentences, shortened reasoning), asks the Gemini API which is better (with confidence and a short reason), and trains an **encoder with a linear head** on these pairs to score (prompt, reasoning) in [0,1]. It rewards the reasoning only, not the answer. Published as a PyTorch Kaggle model.
- **Reward design, 17 functions split by task type and by span.** Format (exact order 0–3, partial 0–3, single occurrence, non-empty sections, no marker leakage between reasoning and answer); reasoning length band; a **reasoning-budget reward (−1.5 to 3) inspired by NVIDIA Nemotron 3's thinking budget**, so verbosity gives diminishing then negative returns; answer-length band per category; domain heuristics (coding: hypothesis → test → fix present, code block in answer; math: derivation signals, a number in the answer; writing: clear intent, minimal); anti-placeholder/refusal penalty; plus the ThoughtTeacher score.
- **Evaluation:** before/after test loop on the reward suite itself, not an external benchmark: mean total reward 6.60 → 10.63 (+61%), its std halved; every category up (writing +88%, reasoning +66%) (writeup). Caveat the author states: the ThoughtTeacher component actually fell (0.090 → 0.080) and "I should have increased the weight of the CoRGI-TT more", so the learned process reward barely shaped training; most of the gain is format and length compliance.
- **Artifacts:** Deep-CoRGI dataset, CoRGI-TT model, data-creation and RM-training notebooks, Gemma2-Deep model, video.

### HM: Gemmatron-Tunix (One_Horizon)
The writeup is prose-only (no numbers); the facts below come mostly from the team's notebook [kkfkmf/gemmatron-final-submission](https://www.kaggle.com/code/kkfkmf/gemmatron-final-submission).
- **Pipeline:** SFT-1 → GRPO → **SFT-2 self-distillation**. SFT-1 teaches the tag format; GRPO with deterministic verifiers improves reasoning; SFT-2 harvests the best-of-N GRPO group rollouts (`best_of_n.jsonl`), ranks by reward, keeps the top traces and fine-tunes on them, the idea being that RL gains are "compressed" into a stable supervised policy that is easier to deploy. This RL-then-distill-back loop is the entry's distinctive move.
- **Model:** Gemma-2-2B-IT loaded from safetensors into Tunix, LoRA rank 32 / alpha 64 for both phases.
- **Data:** SFT 4,095 synthetic examples, average trace 496 words: STEM and coding 33%, creative ideation and writing 25%, general instructions and summarization 20%, **contrastive "unsolvable or ambiguous" prompts 15% (600 examples, teach refusal when premises are flawed)**, atomic logic 5% (Knights and Knaves, LSAT-style). GRPO 1,244 verifiable questions: GMAT reasoning 500 (A–E option extraction), truth-table logic 400, safety/compliance 100 (refusal is the correct state).
- **Hyperparameters:** SFT LR 2e-4, batch 4; GRPO LR 1e-5, batch 1, num_generations 6, β 0.04 ("the sweet spot for maintaining the reasoning format without collapsing the creative search").
- **Reward:** deterministic verifiers only (answer correctness, constraint satisfaction, refusal correctness, format); the team explicitly rejects learned or judge rewards to avoid "sounding smart but wrong".
- **Compute:** notebook budgets ~2 h SFT and ~7 h GRPO (that already fills 9 h before SFT-2, so how SFT-2 fits the single session is unclear). An unrestricted model `kkfkmf/gemmatron` is declared for the optional +15.
- **Evaluation:** "responses to unseen questions" checked for structure and accuracy; **no numbers reported**.

### HM: SFT First, DPO Second: Reasoning Without a Reward (Yuchen20)
The only **no-RL** winner and the clearest argument against GRPO in this field.
- **Recipe:** Gemma 2 2B-IT, two LoRA stages in one Kaggle TPU notebook: **long-context SFT at 8,192 tokens**, then **DPO capped at 4,096 tokens**. Final hyperparameters came from "a small feasibility sweep for TPU memory"; exact LR, rank and steps are in the notebook, not the writeup.
- **Why no RLVR (writeup):** (1) the judged tasks (summaries, creative writing, ideation) have no deterministic verifier; an in-process LLM judge would not fit TPU memory, and an API judge adds non-determinism; (2) the author's GSPO experiments were reward-design-sensitive and reward-hacked (repetitive loops, rambling under a length reward), and RLVR teaches a format less sample-efficiently than SFT inside 9 h. Contrast: 1st place solved the memory objection with a split-mesh on-TPU judge.
- **SFT data:** filtered single-turn subset of `allenai/Dolci-Think-SFT-7B` (`<think>` tags rewritten to `<reasoning>`) plus ~25% `HelpingAI/Dhanishtha-2.0-SUPERTHINKER` for general tasks. Filters: single-turn only, drop malformed tags, rebalance away from math/code-heavy sources, drop multilingual/safety-heavy sources, and **length shaping that raises the share of short direct Q&A** to cure "endless reasoning" that never converges to an answer.
- **DPO data:** filtered `allenai/Dolci-Think-DPO-7B`, where chosen comes from qwen3-reasoning-32b and rejected from qwen3-reasoning-0.6b; the purpose is to iron out mixed-teacher style drift, not to teach correctness.
- **Evaluation:** qualitative only: greedy decoding on a small probe set checking tag compliance, reasoning-answer mismatch and runaway verbosity; the favourite probe was "Tell a short joke about coffee." No held-out numbers.
- **Lesson reported:** an unpinned `pip install git+https://github.com/google/tunix` broke LoRA sharding late in the contest; pin versions.
- **Optional +15:** submitted `yuchen2066/gemma-2-2b-reasoning-mode`.

### HM: Precision Alignment, Crafted in Traces (TraceCraft: Mohammad Shadab Alam, Ng Wei En)
The trainer-surgery entry: **GRPO only, no SFT stage**, on **Gemma3-1B-IT**, starting from the starter notebook.
- **Trainer patch (distinctive):** monkey-patched Tunix's GRPO with **2-GRPO** (group size 2, a contrastive reading of GRPO, arXiv 2510.00977) plus **Dr. GRPO** (removes the length/std normalisation bias toward long wrong answers, arXiv 2503.20783), and, following Dr. GRPO, **disabled the KL term and the reference model** to save compute. Their published model is named `outliar/hacky-drgrpo`, notebook `hacky-no-kl-cl-true`.
- **Data:** 12,961 train / 1,441 eval mixed from GSM8K (7,138), UltraChat (4,901), WritingPrompts (443), MBPP (326), XSum (79), HumanEval (60), HumanEvalPack (13); long-input samples dropped. **gpt-oss-120b** wrote compact "baseline" reasoning traces per sample (JSON-structured for code: task, constraints, pseudocode plan, verify).
- **Reward:** starter-notebook GRPO rewards (format, answer) plus a **cosine-similarity bonus when generated reasoning (and text answers) match the teacher trace above 0.5**, the same "dense similarity to a teacher" idea 3rd place used with TF-IDF.
- **Efficiency:** length-based curriculum (short = easy) and length bucketing to cut padding.
- **Ablation:** {GRPO, 2GRPO+DrGRPO} × {curriculum+bucketing on/off} × LoRA rank {32, 64}; best = 2GRPO+DrGRPO, curriculum on, rank 32. Most compute went to the restricted single-session mode, a fraction to an unrestricted run. No full grid ("time constraints").
- **Evaluation ("expected", restricted mode, own held-out split):** accuracy / format: GSM8K 60.47% / 97.13%, UltraChat 90.35% / 99.07%, WritingPrompts 9.09% / 100%, HumanEval 66.67% / 88.89%, MBPP 87.50% / 93.75%, XSum 22.22% / 100% (writeup). Text "accuracy" is cosine > 0.5 against the reference, so UltraChat's 90% is a soft match; no baseline row is given.
- **Learnings:** small trainer details (advantage and length normalisation, grouping) skew gradients as much as the reward does; teacher-style reasoning rewards need coupling to correctness on math; KL "often hurt when mis-scaled".

### HM: Optimized Tunix (Green)
**The writeup is about 90 words** and says "see notebook"; the details below come from the notebook [green000/final-tunix-notebook](https://www.kaggle.com/code/green000/final-tunix-notebook) and its fork repo `Green0-0/tunix-cce`. 6th-place TheItCrow called this entry "my hidden champ" in 691572.
- **Systems contribution (distinctive):** Tunix full fine-tuning OOM'd above 1,024 context. The author **forked Tunix and added flash attention, cut-cross-entropy and full-model remat** ("four days of vibe coding" with Google Antigravity, no prior JAX), saving "over 100 GB" so FFT fits at **batch 64, context 4,096** on the Kaggle TPU; upstream PR https://github.com/google/tunix/pull/966. Claims the TPU beat his 4×RTX Pro Unsloth DDP setup (notebook).
- **Model choice by measurement:** a quick benchmark harness showed Gemma2-2B "significantly better than Gemma3-1b in almost every way". A first CPT/mid-training run on the base model regressed benchmarks by over 50%, so the final recipe is SFT on top of **gemma-2-2b-it**.
- **Rejects GRPO** outright: "repeatedly shown to not converge for small models", too risky for the budget.
- **Stage 1, full fine-tune (not LoRA):** ~100k packed rows, **~0.5B tokens**, mean 4,000 tokens per row (bin-packing), from `nvidia/Nemotron-Cascade-SFT-Stage-2` and `MegaScience/MegaScience`, keeping rows whose thinking is longer than the response and under 4,096 tokens; pre-tokenised to save TPU time. HF `G-reen/instruct-set-longer-fixed`.
- **Stage 2, SimPO:** ~25k rows (writeup). Prompts pooled from arena/preference sets (PPE-Human-Preference, arena-hard-auto, WildBench, oasst, no_robots, arena-expert-5k, writing-prompts, OREAL-RL, MegaScience), the author openly notes some are evaluation sets; Qwen3-4B-Instruct classifies 8 categories; **GLM 4.7 (NVFP4) writes chosen responses**, with thinking on for math/science/code and, for the rest, **synthetic "fake" thinking traces written from Kimi K2 few-shot examples**, long traces summarised under 4k; rejected from deepseek-distill-llama-8b, qwen3-4b and gemma-2-2b-it. Rationale: raw thinking traces are often irrelevant to the answer and bloated ("wait" five times).
- **Evaluation:** benchmark charts per run (MMLU and ARC drop, as expected; reasoning present); the author admits the eval was not detailed enough to say what SimPO did.
- **Single-session caveat:** the author could not make FFT → SimPO run reliably in one notebook (wandb/HF bugs) and asks judges to fall back to the FFT checkpoint; saves safetensors only (orbax hang). Unrestricted model: 3 epochs on 2 nodes of 4×RTX Pro with **Unsloth on GPUs**, then SimPO.

### HM: Project Glassbox: Teaching Gemma to Think Before It Speaks (OINK BOINK: NamitRana06, Khushboo Kataria)
- **Model:** `google/gemma-2-2b` **base** (not IT), LoRA on q/k/v/o, bf16, single Kaggle TPU; all three tiers claimed to run inside one 9 h session (writeup). Code also on GitHub (Namit-Rana6/GoogleTunixHackathon).
- **Pipeline:** Tier-1 SFT → Tier-2 GRPO → Tier-3 **best-of-N self-distillation** (the same RL-then-distill-back shape as Gemmatron).
- **Data, the "GlassBox" corpus:** ~50k rows balanced as exactly **10k per domain** (coding, science, math, general reasoning, long-context "Big Para" reading/summarisation), from "Sagi" reasoning sets, NVIDIA help/quality sets and DeepSeek-R1 distilled traces, all re-tagged; a 10 to 15 day data sprint. Tier-2 uses a hand-cleaned 5,000-row subset with unambiguous ground truth; Tier-3 draws two non-overlapping unseen subsets to avoid leakage.
- **Reward (rule-based, grew with failure modes):** format gate, exact-match correctness for math/code, then penalties added for **empty reasoning (< 20 characters) and high n-gram repetition** after the model learned `<reasoning></reasoning>` shortcuts and phrase loops. GRPO chosen over PPO because a critic copy of Gemma OOMs.
- **Self-distillation:** ~1,000 prompts × 4 candidates; length filtering plus quality gate keeps only ~10–15%; the survivors are merged with Tier-1 data for a final SFT pass. The writeup titles this "LLM-as-Judge Self-Distillation" but describes no judge model, only gating.
- **Systems:** **dynamic generation-length bounding** from KV-cache capacity, prompt length and a safety margin to avoid OOM; bucketing and padding to avoid XLA recompiles.
- **Evaluation:** holdout math and logic puzzles; "near 100%" format compliance and anecdotal self-correction; **no accuracy numbers or baseline**. Limitations listed honestly (quantifier syllogisms, terse answers, prompt-token leakage cleaned at inference).

### HM: Gemma3Think (Keegan Carey; HF handle chimbiwide)
- **Research-driven data choice:** cites "Small Models Struggle to Learn from Strong Reasoners" (arXiv 2502.12143): sub-3B models learn better from **short, simple traces** than from long R1-style CoT, so the team synthesised its own short traces with **Qwen3-14B** run locally (~70 tok/s on an RTX 5070 Ti).
- **Data:** `chimbiwide/think-32k` (also 20k and 10k versions), 9 categories: general QA 7,706, math 7,473, creative writing 4,709, brainstorming 3,766, science QA 3,000, summarisation 2,188, classification 2,136, information extraction 1,506, code 427; split 20% SFT / 80% RL. **Notable cross-link: the 1st-place G-RaR team used chimbiwide creative and general data in its SFT mix (bank).**
- **SFT:** Gemma3-1b-it, LoRA r32/α64, LR 2e-4 cosine with 10% warmup, batch 2, max length 512, clip 1.0, 1 epoch (~30 min on the 32k set).
- **GRPO:** LoRA r32/α64, LR 5e-6, 15% warmup, β 0.2, ε 0.1, 4 generations, max grad norm 0.5, temperature 1.0, 1 epoch over the RL subset (~7 h on 28k); most development on **Colab TPU v6e-1** because Kaggle TPU queues were long.
- **Reward (distinctive): one category-aware reward**, each category routed to a strategy: semantic (40% **all-mpnet-base-v2** cosine to reference + 15% length + 25% reasoning + 20% format), exact (50/30/20), hybrid, keyword. The embedding scorer runs on CPU in ~20 ms, chosen over an LLM judge (slow, unreliable, competes for TPU) and BERTScore (> 1 s, misses wholly wrong answers).
- **Failure modes reported:** LR 1e-6 and clip 0.02 learned nothing; separate format rewards were saturated after SFT (99% format) so loss sat near 0 and the model did not explore, fixed by folding format into a 20% bonus; a single LoRA adapter across SFT and GRPO (to satisfy Orbax-checkpoint submission) caused catastrophic forgetting of the tags.
- **Evaluation:** none quantitative in the writeup; math and coding acknowledged as "mediocre". Submission notebook name (`gemma-3think-10k-sft-rl`) suggests the 10k variant was used for the single-session run (inference from the URL, UNVERIFIED).

### HM: Does Rubric-Guided Writing Lead to Better Reasoning? (chenchienyi, Pakawat Piampatiparn, Amita01)
The second **Rubrics-as-Rewards** entry (after 1st place), framed as a small paper with a staged ablation on public benchmarks.
- **Model and recipe:** Gemma-3 1B, SFT alignment stage then GRPO; published LoRA `pakawatpiampatiparn/gemma-3-grpo-lora` (JAX). GRPO: 4 rollouts per prompt, LR 3e-6 with 0.1 warmup + cosine, temperature 0.9. RL steps allocated by domain: **GSM8K 5,000, OpenMath 2,000, DeepWriting 1,800**.
- **Data:** OpenMathInstruct-1 and DeepWriting-20K, deduplicated and normalised; 10% formatted for SFT (truncated at 1,024 tokens; DeepWriting reasoning = first 1,500 tokens of the solution with bridging sentences to the answer), the remaining 90% plus GSM8K for RL.
- **Rubrics (distinctive):** an LLM prompt turns each writing task into independent, verifiable criteria, each with title, description and weight, tiered **Essential / Important / Bonus plus negative Penalty rubrics** (a "push-pull" signal). Released as `LibbyChen/deepwriting-rubrics-llama3-2` ("Writing-RaR"), the name implying Llama-3.2 generated them. Which model scores the rubrics during RL is not stated in the text (the formula is an image), so UNVERIFIED.
- **Math reward:** format score plus content score with a **dynamic discount**: content counts 100% only when correctness clears a threshold, to stop "right answer for the wrong reasons". Writing reward weights the normalised rubric score above format.
- **Evaluation (the most benchmark-like of the HMs):** greedy, MATH-500 0-shot / HellaSwag 2-shot: base 6.2 / 34.6; +math RL 17.4 / 37.2; +DeepWriting 19.2 / 35.4; +1,600 more writing steps 28.0 / 38.6, i.e. +21.8 and +4.0 (writeup). The claim that more rubric-guided *writing* RL raised *math* accuracy is the title's thesis; there is one seed and no control for simply training longer.
- Compute and single-session fit are not described.

### HM: Show Your Work: 4-Tier Hybrid Process Rewards (heissanjay / sanjay)
- **Recipe:** **GRPO only, no SFT**, Gemma2 2B-IT, LoRA rank 64 / alpha 128 on attention and MLP; 4 generations, β 0.04, ε 0.2, peak LR 3e-6 cosine, clip 0.1, **1,125 steps, batch 4**, TPU v5e-8 with a **(2,4) FSDP + TP mesh**, "completed within 9-hour TPU session budget" (writeup).
- **Reward, four tiers (distinctive as an API-judge process reward):** (1) format +0.5, deliberately weak; (2) **reasoning quality ±1.0 from Gemini 2.0 Flash** scoring five rubrics (logical coherence 30%, completeness 25%, groundedness 20%, non-circularity 15%, clarity 10%) at 0 / 0.5 / 1, rescaled to [−1, 1]; (3) answer correctness ±1.0, deterministic for math/science, Gemini-judged (relevance, completeness, clarity, helpfulness, tone) for open tasks; (4) conciseness by word count (768–1,024 words +1.0, 256–768 +0.7, 128–256 +0.5, else −1.0).
- **Judge engineering:** 30-worker ThreadPool with a 20-request semaphore, MD5 response cache (85%+ hit rate by mid-training, 80%+ API cost cut), exponential backoff; failed calls ~15% → < 1%; temperature 0 judge variance < 2% on repeats; full validation in ~2 min instead of ~15 (writeup).
- **Data:** 5,000 samples (4,500 train / 500 val), 70% non-verifiable by design: OpenOrca 1,750, WritingPrompts 1,250, SciQ 1,000, GSM8K 500, HH-RLHF 500.
- **Failure mode reported:** the model learned the format within tens of samples while loss sat near 1.0, i.e. it wrapped base-model answers in tags; fixed by switching regex `.match` to `.search` (trailing `<end_of_turn>` caused false negatives), rebalancing format vs quality, and minimum length/complexity gates before positive reward.
- **Evaluation:** no before/after accuracy or win-rate numbers; claims are about judge consistency and infrastructure.

### HM: DOD Google Tunix Hack (DoD: SangminLee09, kimsunki_12)
- **Recipe:** Gemma-2-2B-IT, **two-stage LoRA SFT** then LoRA GRPO; published model `sangminlee09/dod-gemma2-2b-it-reasoning-800`; code and figures at GitHub `san9min/tunix-hack`. Hyperparameters live in the notebook, not the writeup.
- **SFT data:** curated from `glaiveai/reasoning-v1-20m` (general-domain traces) and `bespokelabs/Bespoke-Stratos-17k` (correctness-filtered math/code/science/logic), English only, tag-normalised, token-budgeted; sampled at a **1:4 long-CoT to short-CoT ratio**. Stage 2 keeps the subset with reasoning under 1,024 tokens and slightly more math, to consolidate format under the length cap. ~20k-scale dataset (writeup's lesson section).
- **RL data:** numeric-answer problems from `knoveleng/open-rs` (integers, decimals, fractions incl. LaTeX `\frac`), creative tasks from `SAA-Lab/LitBench-Train` chosen by user-feedback signals, non-creative science/business reasoning from `openbmb/RLPR-Train-Dataset`; difficulty stratified 1:3 easy:hard.
- **Reward (distinctive): domain-routed hybrid.** Math: rule-based format + normalised/numeric answer match with partial credit for near-misses. Non-math: an **RLPR-style probability reward**, the policy's own likelihood of the reference answer under teacher forcing after its reasoning, **minus a prompt-only baseline** to remove prompt bias, with low-variance prompts filtered out. This gives a dense signal for non-verifiable tasks **without any judge model**, the cheapest answer in the field to the "how do you reward creative writing" question.
- **Lessons (writeup):** pure long CoT makes a small LoRA-SFT model overthink easy GSM8K items and still miss; pure short CoT caps capability; mixing is needed before RL. Tune the data mixture by watching GSM8K accuracy (as a math canary) and format accuracy. Concise reasoning comes from staged exposure (rich → learnable length → RL under a strict budget), not from truncation.
- **Evaluation:** GSM8K and format accuracy used as monitors; no final numbers in the writeup.

## Consensus vs divergence

**Table stakes.** A Gemma 2 2B IT or Gemma 3 1B policy with LoRA; traces distilled from a much larger model and re-tagged into the required XML; a format reward plus a verifiable math reward (GSM8K appears in most RL mixes); a public notebook, public datasets and a video. Nearly every writeup names the same failure: once SFT teaches the format, format rewards saturate and stop giving signal (Gemma3Think, 4-tier, 1st).

**What separated the six placed entries from the HMs** (host reasons in 691572 plus the evidence): (a) a reward for non-verifiable tasks that is both dense and cheap: on-TPU judge (1st), async API judge (2nd), TF-IDF to teacher (3rd), OPD token-level supervision (5th), learned process RM (6th); 4th is the exception, judged on model quality; (b) multi-stage pipelines (2nd and 4th both run SFT → preference → GRPO; 5th runs five stages across teacher and student); (c) quantitative evaluation with a baseline or ablation in 1st to 5th, versus mostly qualitative evaluation among HMs; (d) explicit single-session engineering (split mesh, per-algorithm mesh choice, chained checkpoints). The HMs often had one strong idea (Green's Tunix fork, DoD's likelihood reward, TraceCraft's trainer patch, Rubric HM's benchmark gains) but thinner evidence or a single-session caveat.

**4th place is the outlier worth noting:** the shortest writeup in the field (9 words) and no reported metric, but the host cited "exceptional model quality". Model quality is 45 of 100 points, judged on a held-out set nobody saw, so a clean SFT → DPO → GRPO run with a Gemini-ranked, failure-mined DPO set beat many better-documented entries.

**Contradictions:**
- *Is GRPO worth it for a 1–2B model in 9 h?* No: Yuchen20 (GSPO reward-hacked; SFT is more sample-efficient) [writeup](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/google-tunix-hackathon/writeups/new-writeup-1763082571151) and Green ("repeatedly shown to not converge for small models") [writeup](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/google-tunix-hackathon/writeups/optimized-tunix). Yes: 13 winners including all six placed entries.
- *Can an LLM judge fit?* Yuchen20 says an in-process judge cannot fit TPU memory and an API judge is non-deterministic; 1st place fits a 12B judge on the same v5e-8 via split mesh [writeup](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/google-tunix-hackathon/writeups/new-writeup-1768107128907); 4-tier reports < 2% API-judge variance at temperature 0 [writeup](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/google-tunix-hackathon/writeups/new-writeup-1766324142382); Gemma3Think calls LLM judges "very unreliable" [writeup](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/google-tunix-hackathon/writeups/gemma3think).
- *KL penalty:* TraceCraft dropped KL and the reference model entirely (Dr. GRPO) and says KL "often hurt"; 1st, 4th, 6th, Gemmatron and Gemma3Think keep a reference model (β from 0.04 to 0.2).
- *Long vs short CoT for SFT:* Gemma3Think goes short-only (Qwen3-14B short traces); Green trains on long DeepSeek-style traces (0.5B tokens) but summarises preference traces; DoD finds a 1:4 long:short mix is needed; Yuchen20 oversamples short Q&A to stop endless reasoning.
- *SFT before RL "mandatory"?* 1st place argues yes; 3rd place reached the podium with GRPO from the IT model plus a teacher-similarity reward and no SFT stage (per its writeup).
